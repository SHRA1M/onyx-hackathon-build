## gap-4: automated disconnection review

**Clauses searched**

**Global example**

**Recommendation.** Cabinet to set a general duty to tell a consumer, at the first interaction, that they are talking to an AI, beyond the sales case of 11(2).

## gap-7: five mentions no standard

No binding UAE text sets conduct standards for an AI agent dealing with a consumer. The clauses that name AI or an automated agent set a charge waiver (CPR 25.14.1), a bar (26.7.2), a definition (Spam Calls v4 3.3(iii)), a permission (Cabinet 56/2024 5(6)) and a tool (Child Digital Safety 10(1)(g)); PDPL 18 and Electronic Transactions 11 to 13 govern automated decisions and contracts as outcomes, not the agent's conduct.

Kind: absence. Category: standards gap. Addressee: TDRA. Exposed by: all.

**Clauses searched**

- `tdra-cpr-v2-en:25.14.1` (p. 64): Directory enquiries by an AIA: charges waived if it fails. A billing rule, not a conduct standard.
  - EN: "enquiry is processed by an artificial intelligence assistance (AIA)"
  - AR (the whole chunk): "معالجة استفسار المستهلك بواسطة مساعد ذكاء اصطناعي ((AIA؛ و"
- `tdra-cpr-v2-en:26.7.2` (p. 65): Non-human agents excluded from complaint conversations. A bar, not a conduct standard.
  - EN: "preclude the use of non-human agents by Licensees in conversations with Consumers regarding Consumer Complaints."
  - AR (the whole chunk): "يستثنى أو يستبعد منها استخدام وكلاء غير بشريين من قبل المرخص لهم في المحادثات مع المستهلكين بشأن الشكاوى المقدمة من قبلهم."
- `spam-calls-v4-en:3.3` (p. 7): Point (iii): a synthesised voice can make a Spam Call. A definition.
  - EN: "the audible message contains a natural or a synthesised voice"
- `cabinet-56-2024-en:5(6)` (p. 5): Automated systems may market. A permission.
  - EN: "Automated communication systems may be used for marketing"
- `child-digital-safety-26-2025-en:10(1)` (p. 10): Point (g): platforms may use AI to detect harmful content. A tool, not a standard for AI conduct.
  - EN: "including artificial intelligence systems and machine learning algorithms, for proactive detection, removal, or reporting thereof"
- `pdpl-45-2021-en:18(4)` (p. 22): The controller adds a human to the review of an automated processing decision when the data subject asks. A rule for the decision, not for the agent's conduct.
  - EN: "The Controller shall include the human element in reviewing automated processing decisions at the request of the Data Subject."
- `etransactions-46-2021-en:11(2)` (p. 17): An automated system can make a contract if the other person knows, or should know, it is automated. A rule for the contract, not for the agent's conduct.
  - EN: "if the latter knows, or is supposed to know, that such system will make or execute the contract automatically."
- `etransactions-46-2021-en:12(2)` (p. 17): A document sent by an automated medium on the originator's behalf is the originator's. Attribution of the outcome, not a conduct standard.
  - EN: "If it is sent by an electronic medium automated and programmed to operate automatically by or on behalf of the Originator."
- `cabinet-57-2024-en:7` (p. 5): Table No. (1), row 16: a fine for using automatic calling or marketing in breach of Cabinet 56/2024 (Clause (6)). A penalty, not a conduct standard.
  - EN: "16 Using automatic calling or marketing"

**UAE position.** The binding texts read name AI or an automated agent to set a charge waiver, a bar, a definition, a permission and a tool; none sets how an AI agent must behave with a consumer. PDPL 18(4), Electronic Transactions 11(2) and 12(2) and Cabinet 57/2024 Table No. (1) row 16 govern automated decisions, contracts, documents and calls as outcomes, not the agent's conduct.

**Global example**

- IMDA Model AI Governance Framework for Agentic AI v1.5, 2.1.2 (VERIFIED, `kb/sources/global/imda-agentic-ai-v1.5.txt`): "In general, prefer deterministic rather than non-deterministic limits, and bound by design."
- IMDA Model AI Governance Framework for Agentic AI v1.5, 2.1.2 (VERIFIED, `kb/sources/global/imda-agentic-ai-v1.5.txt`): "rather than relying on prompts to instruct the agent against accessing certain tools, impose access controls that prevent the tool from being called by the agent at all."

**Recommendation.** TDRA to set conduct standards for AI agents in consumer care (limits, oversight, logs), as IMDA's framework does as a whole; Mizan's gates are one worked form of them.

## gap-8: Y.3172 has no hand-over to a human

Standards gap, addressed to ITU: Y.3172 has no node outcome for a hand-over to a human. The policy node applies policies to the model's output and the distributor sends it on to a sink that acts; no node routes it to a person.

Kind: absence. Category: standards gap. Addressee: ITU. Exposed by: S2-3.

**Clauses searched**

- `itu-t-y3172:8.1` (p. 13 to 14): Clause 8.1 (pages 13 and 14) defines the nodes SRC, C, PP, M, P, D and SINK. P applies policies to the model's output; D distributes it to SINK nodes, which act on it. No node or outcome sends an output to a human.
  - EN: "P (policy): This node enables the application of policies to the output of the model node."
- `itu-t-y3172:7.1` (p. 4): The only mention of a human in the Recommendation's text (page 4): a SON example acting 'without human intervention'. The back-page list of ITU-T series names 'human factors' as a series title only.
  - EN: "without human intervention"

**UAE position.** CPR 26.7.2 and licence 9.1 require a human in a complaint conversation; Mizan's P node gate returns a hand-over that Y.3172 has no outcome for, so it is mapped outside the standard's node set.

**The standard's own text**

- ITU-T Y.3172 (06/2019), 8.1, page 14 (VERIFIED, `kb/sources/global/itu-t-y3172-2019-06.txt`): "P (policy): This node enables the application of policies to the output of the model node."
- ITU-T Y.3172 (06/2019), 8.1, page 14 (VERIFIED, `kb/sources/global/itu-t-y3172-2019-06.txt`): "D (distributor): This node is responsible for identifying the SINK(s) and distributing the output of the M node to the corresponding SINK nodes."
- ITU-T Y.3172 (06/2019), 8.1, page 14 (VERIFIED, `kb/sources/global/itu-t-y3172-2019-06.txt`): "SINK: This node is the target of the ML output on which it takes action."
- ITU-T Y.3172 (06/2019), 7.1, page 4 (VERIFIED, `kb/sources/global/itu-t-y3172-2019-06.txt`): "give network design recommendations without human intervention."

**Global example.** none; the recommendation is the example

**Recommendation.** ITU-T to add a policy-node outcome that withholds the model's output and routes the case to a named human (hand-over), with its own reference point, so a regulator's human-in-the-loop rule maps into the pipeline.

## gap-9: a child on the line

A child on the line: the Child Digital Safety Law 26/2025 makes the caregiver sign the service terms where the beneficiary is a child (11(1)(b)) and requires verified caregiver consent for under-13 data (7(1)), but the Cabinet classification (Art 6), the TDRA instructions to providers (Art 11) and the penalties (Art 16) are unpublished, and no text addressed to a telecom provider says how an automated channel learns that the person is a child.

Kind: method. Category: legislative gap. Addressee: TDRA, Cabinet. Exposed by: mapping (SRC, P); U2 (drill bank, not run).

**Clauses searched**

- `child-digital-safety-26-2025-en:7(1)` (p. 8): No under-13 data without the caregiver's consent, explicit, documented and verifiable; no method of verifying it is named.
  - EN: "Obtaining parental consent from the child caregiver, provided that such consent is explicit, documented, and verifiable."
- `child-digital-safety-26-2025-en:9(2)` (p. 10): Platforms and Internet Service Providers must keep children from prohibited games, age verification included; no mechanism is named.
  - EN: "including age-verification mechanisms, Parental Control Tools, and blocking Harmful Content."
- `child-digital-safety-26-2025-en:10(1)` (p. 10): Point (g): platforms may use AI systems to detect, remove or report harmful content. A tool, not an age signal; point (b)'s age verification is asked of platforms, by their Art 6 class.
  - EN: "including artificial intelligence systems and machine learning algorithms, for proactive detection, removal, or reporting thereof"
- `child-digital-safety-26-2025-en:11(1)` (p. 12): Point (b): the TDRA instructions to Internet Service Providers include the caregiver signing the service terms where the beneficiary is a child; the instructions are unpublished.
  - EN: "including requiring the Child Caregiver to sign service terms"
  - AR (`docs/sources/child-digital-safety-law-26-2025-ar.txt`): "إلزام القائم على رعاية الطفل بالتوقيع على شروط الخدمة"
- `child-digital-safety-26-2025-en:11(1)` (p. 12): Paragraph 2 (11(2), held in the 11(1) chunk): TDRA reviews the providers' policies and supervises their compliance.
  - EN: "The Authority shall review the policies of Internet Service Providers and supervise and monitor their compliance"
- `tdra-cpr-v2-en:4.3` (p. 14): No Service the Subscriber has not expressly ordered; no age condition on who orders.
  - EN: "Licensees shall not register, charge or otherwise avail a Service to a Subscriber that the Subscriber has not expressly ordered or opted to pay for."
  - AR (the whole chunk): "مع مراعاة أحكام المادة الفرعية 4.4 ، يجب ألا يقوم المرخص لهم بتسجيل أو استيفاء رسوم أو جعل المشترك مستفيداً من خدمة لم يطلبها المشترك أو يختر الدفع لقائها صراحةً."
- `tdra-cpr-v2-en:4.7` (p. 14): The consent provisions for receiving or paying for a Service follow; none sets an age condition on a sale.
  - EN: "with reference to a Subscriber"
  - AR (the whole chunk): ":في سياق هذه الأنظمة وبالرجوع إلى موافقة المشترك على تلقي الخدمة و/أو الدفع مقابلها، يتم تطبيق الأحكام التالية"

**UAE position.** The duty is the law's own words; the operator's licence 3.2 makes it a licence condition; the instruments that would set the age signal, the caregiver check and the penalties do not exist yet (searched 2026-10-16 and 2026-10-22, both languages). Two published texts come near and neither reaches a telecom channel. Cabinet Resolution 106/2026 (issued 2026-06-17, in force 2026-06-30) 'shall apply to all Social Media Platforms whose services are made available within the State or directed at users therein' (Art 2); it lists age-verification mechanisms for those platforms (Art 4(2)) and says 'Self-declaration of age ... shall not be recognized' (Art 4(3)), but its standards are still to be 'approved by the Child Digital Safety Council upon the proposal of the Telecommunications and Digital Government Regulatory Authority' (Arts 3(4)(c), 4(1)(h), 4(2)(d)) and its penalties are those 'prescribed under the list of administrative penalties issued pursuant to the provisions of the Decree by Law' (Art 7(1), 7(2)), the Art 16 list that is not published. TDRA's 'Telecom for Minors' page (consumer hub, updated 2026-10-01) describes an initiative launched 'in collaboration with licensed telecommunications service providers' that 'Supports the goals of the UAE child digital safety law'; its terms 'are determined by each service provider', the links 'are provided for consumer information only', and it is not on the TDRA Regulations and Ruling register.

**Global example**

- US COPPA Rule, 16 CFR 312.5 (Parental consent), 312.5(a)(1) (VERIFIED, `kb/sources/global/coppa-16cfr312-5.txt`): "obtain verifiable parental consent before any collection, use, or disclosure of personal information from children"
- US COPPA Rule, 16 CFR 312.5 (Parental consent), 312.5(b)(1) (VERIFIED, `kb/sources/global/coppa-16cfr312-5.txt`): "reasonably calculated, in light of available technology, to ensure that the person providing consent is the child's parent"

**Recommendation.** TDRA to issue the Art 11 instructions with the caregiver check an automated channel must run before activating or changing a service, and the consent method for under-13 data. Resolution 106 Art 4(2) already lists acceptable age-verification mechanisms for social media platforms, and 16 CFR 312.5(b) lists acceptable consent methods; the Art 11 instructions can name a list of the same shape for telecom channels.
